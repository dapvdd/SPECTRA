'''Meteor Lake''' is the [[List of Intel codenames|codename]] for Core Ultra Series 1 mobile processors, designed by [[Intel]]<ref name="Gomes-2022">{{cite book |last1=Gomes |first1=Wilfred |last2=Morgan |first2=Slade |last3=Phelps |first3=Boyd |last4=Wilson |first4=Tim |last5=Hallnor |first5=Erik |doi=10.1109/HCS55958.2022.9895532 |chapter=Meteor Lake and Arrow Lake Intel Next-Gen 3D Client Architecture Platform with Foveros |title=2022 IEEE Hot Chips 34 Symposium (HCS) |date=2022 |pages=1–40 |isbn=978-1-6654-6028-6 |s2cid=252551808 }}</ref> and officially released on December 14, 2023.<ref name="Intel-AI-PC-2023">{{cite web|url=https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/news/core-ultra-client-computing-news-1.html|title=Intel Core Ultra Ushers in the Age of the AI PC|website=Intel|date=December 14, 2023 |access-date=2026-05-27|archive-date=2023-12-14|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231214150505/https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/news/core-ultra-client-computing-news-1.html|url-status=live}}</ref> It is the first generation of Intel mobile processors to use a [[chiplet]] architecture which means that the processor is a multi-chip module.<ref name="Gomes-2022"/> Meteor Lake's design effort was led by Tim Wilson.<ref name="Intel-Corporation-SoC-40-years-2004">{{Cite web |title=The 'Blank Sheet' that Delivered Intel's Most Significant SoC Design Change in 40 Years |url=https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/news/blank-sheet-opens-intels-new-soc-design.html |website=Intel |language=en-US |date=2024-01-17 |access-date=2026-05-27}}</ref>

== Background ==
In July 2021, Meteor Lake was initially announced to be coming with a 5–125W TDP range for various segments ranging from ultra low power mobile to enthusiast desktop.<ref name="Liu-2021">{{cite web |last=Liu |first=Zhiye |date=2021-07-27 |title=Intel Teases 14th-Gen Meteor Lake CPUs With Tile Design and 192 EUs |url=https://www.tomshardware.com/news/intel-teases-14th-gen-meteor-lake-cpus-tile-design-192-eus |website=Tom's Hardware |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-04-05 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240405141306/https://www.tomshardware.com/news/intel-teases-14th-gen-meteor-lake-cpus-tile-design-192-eus |url-status=live }}</ref> The initial tape-in process for Meteor Lake took place in May 2021. The CPU compute tile was confirmed to be fabricated on Intel's 7 nm process (since rebranded to "Intel 4").<ref name="VideoCardz-2021">{{cite web |title=Intel's 7nm "Meteor Lake" compute tile has taped in |url=https://videocardz.com/newz/intels-7nm-meteor-lake-compute-tile-has-taped-in |website=VideoCardz |language=en-US |date=2021-05-24 |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-04-05 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240405141307/https://videocardz.com/newz/intels-7nm-meteor-lake-compute-tile-has-taped-in |url-status=live }}</ref>

In October 2021, Intel said in an earnings call that it had taped out the CPU compute tile for Meteor Lake and after it was received it had powered on within 30 minutes and with expected performance levels.<ref name="Shilov-2021">{{cite web |last=Shilov |first=Anton |date=2021-10-22 |title=Intel: 14th Gen Meteor Lake Compute Tile Powers On, Performs Well |url=https://www.tomshardware.com/news/intel-meteor-lake-powered-on |website=Tom's Hardware |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-04-05 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240405141306/https://www.tomshardware.com/news/intel-meteor-lake-powered-on |url-status=live }}</ref> In April 2022, Intel announced that an assembled Meteor Lake mobile processor had been powered-on for the first time in a development milestone.<ref name="VideoCardz-2022">{{cite web |title=14th Gen Core "Meteor Lake" has been powered-on, on track to launch in 2023 |url=https://videocardz.com/newz/14th-gen-core-meteor-lake-has-been-powered-on-on-track-to-launch-in-2023 |website=VideoCardz |language=en-US |date=2022-04-29 |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-04-05 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240405141305/https://videocardz.com/newz/14th-gen-core-meteor-lake-has-been-powered-on-on-track-to-launch-in-2023 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="Mujtaba-2022">{{cite web |last=Mujtaba |first=Hassan |date=2022-04-29 |title=Intel Achieves 14th Gen Meteor Lake CPU 'Power-On' With Launch Scheduled For 2023 |url=https://wccftech.com/intel-achieves-14th-gen-meteor-lake-cpu-power-on-with-launch-scheduled-for-2023/ |website=Wccftech |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-04-05 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240405141307/https://wccftech.com/intel-achieves-14th-gen-meteor-lake-cpu-power-on-with-launch-scheduled-for-2023/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

In March 2023, it was reported that Intel had decided to cancel development of high-end Meteor Lake-S processors for desktop.<ref name="Norem-2023">{{cite web |last=Norem |first=Josh |date=2023-03-17 |title=Rumor: Intel to Cancel Meteor Lake Desktop, Replace It With Arrow Lake |url=https://www.extremetech.com/computing/rumor-intel-to-cancel-meteor-lake-desktop-replace-it-with-arrow-lake |website=ExtremeTech |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-04-05 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240405141305/https://www.extremetech.com/computing/rumor-intel-to-cancel-meteor-lake-desktop-replace-it-with-arrow-lake |url-status=live }}</ref> Meteor Lake-S processors were being designed to fit into the [[LGA 1851]] socket, which is identical in dimensions to LGA 1700, but the cancellation of desktop Meteor Lake meant that the LGA 1851 socket wouldn't debut until Arrow Lake in 2024.<ref name="VideoCardz-2023">{{cite web |title=Intel's 2024 Arrow Lake-S desktop CPUs to feature up to 24 cores and support DDR5-6400 memory |url=https://videocardz.com/newz/intels-2024-arrow-lake-s-desktop-cpus-to-feature-up-to-24-cores-and-support-ddr5-6400-memory |website=VideoCardz |language=en-US |date=2023-03-17 |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-04-05 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240405141308/https://videocardz.com/newz/intels-2024-arrow-lake-s-desktop-cpus-to-feature-up-to-24-cores-and-support-ddr5-6400-memory |url-status=live }}</ref> The top Meteor Lake-S SKU in development contained 6 Redwood Cove P-cores and 16 Crestmont E-cores, which is two fewer P-cores than the last generation Raptor Lake Core i9-13900K.

At Intel's Innovation event in September 2023, head of Intel's Client Computing Group Michelle Johnston Holthaus confirmed that some Meteor Lake-based processors would come to desktop in 2024. === Branding ===
Intel announced new branding in June 2023 for upcoming Meteor Lake processors after using the same "Core i" branding for over 15 years. Core branding would be simplified by dropping the 'i' with processors branded Core 3, 5 and 7 instead.<ref name="Robinson-2023">{{cite web |last=Robinson |first=Cliff |date=2023-06-15 |title=Intel Overhauls Core Branding with Meteor Lake |url=https://www.servethehome.com/intel-overhauls-core-branding-with-meteor-lake/ |website=ServeTheHome |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-04-05 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240405141541/https://www.servethehome.com/intel-overhauls-core-branding-with-meteor-lake/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The new 'Core Ultra' 5, 7 and 9 branding would be reserved for "premium" processors according to Intel.<ref name="Bonshor-2023a-Intel-to-Launch">{{cite web |last=Bonshor |first=Gavin |date=2023-06-15 |title=Intel To Launch New Core Processor Branding for Meteor Lake: Drop the i, Add Ultra Tier |url=https://www.anandtech.com/show/18911/intel-new-core-branding-for-meteor-lake-no-i-new-ultra-tier |website=AnandTech |language=en-US |access-date=2024-04-05 |archive-date=2024-04-05 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240405141541/https://www.anandtech.com/show/18911/intel-new-core-branding-for-meteor-lake-no-i-new-ultra-tier |url-status=dead }}</ref> In addition to the new tier naming, Intel said it would be de-emphasizing processor generations in marketing material, though the processor generation number would remain in the processor number.<ref name="Thubron-2023">{{cite web |last=Thubron |first=Rob |date=2023-06-15 |title=Intel announces biggest processor rebranding in 15 years ahead of Meteor Lake launch processors with Core Ultra branding are classified as first generation Core Ultra.

== Architecture ==
=== Packaging ===
Meteor Lake is a 64-bit [[x86]] CPU architecture designed around low power operation and increased power efficiency over Raptor Lake. Meteor Lake is the first Intel microarchitecture to utilize a disaggregated multi-chip module (MCM) approach rather than using large monolithic silicon dies. Previously, in June 2017, Intel had derided [[AMD]]'s disaggregated chiplet approach in their [[Ryzen]] and [[Epyc]] processors as using "glued-together" dies.<ref>{{cite web |last=Lilly |first=Paul |date=2017-07-13 |title=Intel slide criticizes AMD for using "glued-together" dies in Epyc processors |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/intel-slide-criticizes-amd-for-using-glued-together-dies-in-epyc-processors/ |website=PC Gamer |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-07-16 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240716124229/https://www.pcgamer.com/intel-slide-criticizes-amd-for-using-glued-together-dies-in-epyc-processors/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

The first advantage of using smaller [[Die (integrated circuit)|dies]] in an MCM is how it brings better modularity and fabricating smaller dies increases silicon yield rates as more dies can be fitted onto a single 300mm [[Wafer (electronics)|wafer]]. As a result of greater yields, the use of multiple pre-tested components in an MCM removes the need for [[Product binning#Semiconductor manufacturing|binning]] an entire assembled CPU as is the case with monolithic dies.<ref>{{cite web |last=Westover |first=Brian |date=2023-09-19 |title='Meteor Lake' Architecture Detailed: What to Know About Intel's Next-Gen CPUs |url=https://uk.pcmag.com/laptops/148712/meteor-lake-architecture-detailed-what-to-know-about-intels-next-gen-cpus |website=PCMag |language=en-GB |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-07-16 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240716124230/https://www.pcmag.com/news/meteor-lake-architecture-detailed-what-to-know-about-intels-next-gen-cpus |url-status=live }}</ref> For example, Raptor Lake desktop silicon with defective graphics is binned into F SKUs with the integrated graphics disabled so they can still be sold while non-F SKUs have their integrated graphics enabled. Instead, Intel can assemble Meteor Lake CPUs using multiple pieces of fully functional silicon while any silicon wafer defects can be discarded entirely. The second advantage is greater flexibility in the use of process nodes. The various dies in an MCM can be fabricated on different nodes depending on their use case. Certain functions like SRAM and general I/O do not linearly scale as logic does with advancements in process node. For example, an I/O die can use a cheaper, more mature process like [[TSMC]]'s [[7 nm process|N6]] while the CPU die can use a more expensive, advanced node like [[5 nm process|N5]] or [[3 nm process|N3]] for greater power efficiency and frequency.

=== Process technology ===
Due to its MCM construction, Meteor Lake can take advantage of different process nodes that are best suited to the use case. Meteor Lake is built using four different fabrication nodes, including both Intel's own nodes and external nodes outsourced to fabrication competitor [[TSMC]]. The "Intel 4" process used for the CPU tile is the first process node in which Intel is utilising [[Extreme ultraviolet lithography|extreme ultraviolet (EUV)]] lithography, which is necessary for creating nodes 7 nm and smaller. The interposer base tile is fabricated on Intel's 22FFL, or "Intel 16", process.<ref>{{cite web |last=Temsamani |first=Fahd |date=2022-08-24 |title=Intel reveals key details about 3D Foveros chip design on Meteor Lake |url=https://www.club386.com/intel-reveals-key-details-about-3d-foveros-chip-design-on-meteor-lake/ |website=Club386 |language=en-GB |access-date=2026-05-17 |archive-date=2024-05-23 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240523145155/https://www.club386.com/intel-reveals-key-details-about-3d-foveros-chip-design-on-meteor-lake/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Deutscher |first=Maria |date=2022-07-25 |title=Intel to produce chips for MediaTek as part of new partnership |url=https://siliconangle.com/2022/07/25/intel-produce-chips-mediatek-part-new-partnership/ |website=Silicon Angle |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-07-16 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240716124232/https://siliconangle.com/2022/07/25/intel-produce-chips-mediatek-part-new-partnership/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The 22FFL (FinFET Low-power) node, first announced in March 2017, was designed for inexpensive low power operation.<ref>{{cite web |last=Mehta |first=Rich |date=2019-02-05 |title=Intel announces tweaks to 22FFL process for RF, MRAM at IEDM18 |url=https://www.semiconductor-digest.com/intel-announces-tweaks-to-22ffl-process-for-rf-mram-at-iedm18/ |website=Semiconductor Digest |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-07-16 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240716124233/https://www.semiconductor-digest.com/intel-announces-tweaks-to-22ffl-process-for-rf-mram-at-iedm18/ |url-status=live }}</ref> The interposer base tile is designed to connect tiles together and allow for die-to-die communication which does not require the most advanced, expensive nodes so an older, inexpensive node can be used instead.

=== Compute tile ===
Meteor Lake's CPU compute tile features up to 6 Redwood Cove P-cores and 8 Crestmont E-cores. Each Redwood Cove P-core features SMT with two threads per core while Crestmont E-cores are limited to one thread per core. The 8 total Crestmont E-cores are organized into two 4-core clusters with shared L2 and L3 caches for each cluster. Each Crestmont E-core cluster has 2{{nbsp}}MB of L2 cache, the same as a Gracemont E-core cluster. Crestmont maintains the same 6-wide out-of-order core design as Gracemont with enhancements to its pipeline. === Graphics tile ===
The dedicated graphics tile in Meteor Lake is fabricated using [[TSMC]]'s [[5 nm process|N5]] node. Contained within the graphics tile are up to 8 X<sup>e</sup>-LPG graphics cores based on the Alchemist architecture with optimizations for low-power. Intel's [[Intel Arc|Arc]] A-series discrete graphics cards use X<sup>e</sup>-HPG cores that are also based on the same Alchemist architecture. Each X<sup>e</sup> core has 16 X<sup>e</sup> Vector Engines (XVEs), giving a total of 128 XVEs across the 8 X<sup>e</sup>-LPG cores. This 128 XVE configuration is a downgrade from the 192 XVEs Intel originally showed for Meteor Lake's graphics in a July 2021 presentation slide.<ref name="Liu-2021" /> The move to the Alchemist architecture also brings the addition of up to 8 ray tracing units, one in each X<sup>e</sup>-LPG core.<ref name="Walton">{{cite web |last=Walton |first=Jarred |date=2023-09-19 |title=Intel's Meteor Lake GPU Doubles Integrated Graphics Performance Per Watt |url=https://www.tomshardware.com/news/intel-meteor-lake-integrated-graphics-doubles-performance-per-watt |website=Tom's Hardware |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-05-23 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240523145155/https://www.tomshardware.com/news/intel-meteor-lake-integrated-graphics-doubles-performance-per-watt |url-status=live }}</ref> Much like the X<sup>e</sup>-HPG variant, each X<sup>e</sup>-LPG core contains a 192{{nbsp}}KB L1 cache shared between all 16 XVEs. The 8 X<sup>e</sup>-LPG cores have access to a 4{{nbsp}}MB global L2 cache.<ref>{{cite web |title=Intel's Ambitious Meteor Lake iGPU |url=https://chipsandcheese.com/2024/04/08/intels-ambitious-meteor-lake-igpu/ |website=Chips and Cheese |language=en-US |date=2024-04-08 |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-05-23 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240523145155/https://chipsandcheese.com/2024/04/08/intels-ambitious-meteor-lake-igpu/ |url-status=live }}</ref> However, what the graphics tile is missing from the Alchemist architecture are X<sup>e</sup> Matrix Extensions (XMX) units. XMX units perform in-silicon AI acceleration, similar to [[Nvidia]]'s Tensor cores. The lack of XMX units means that the X<sup>e</sup>-LPG core instead uses DP4a instructions in line with Microsoft Shader Model 6.4.<ref>{{cite web |last=Cunningham |first=Andrew |date=2023-09-20 |title=Intel's biggest laptop CPU update in years is a huge departure from past designs |url=https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2023/09/intels-meteor-lake-cpus-bring-a-much-faster-gpu-radically-new-chip-design/ |website=Ars Technica |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-05-23 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240523145155/https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2023/09/intels-meteor-lake-cpus-bring-a-much-faster-gpu-radically-new-chip-design/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Meteor Lake's graphics capabilities are greatly increased over the previous generation UHD and Iris Xe integrated graphics in Raptor Lake. Intel claims that Meteor Lake's GPU achieves a 2x increase in performance-per-watt over the Iris Xe graphics featured in Alder Lake and Raptor Lake processors.<ref name="Walton"/> The graphics tile is able to run at much higher clock speeds compared to Intel's previous integrated graphics in Alder Lake and Raptor Lake. Intel claims that Meteor Lake's GPU can "run at a much lower minimum voltage" and hit boost clock speeds of over 2.0{{nbsp}}GHz.<ref>{{cite web |last=Hollister |first=Sean |date=2023-09-19 |title=Intel will launch Meteor Lake on December 14th — this is Intel's Core Ultra |url=https://www.theverge.com/2023/9/19/23872888/intel-meteor-lake-core-ultra-date-chip-specs-details |website=The Verge |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2023-10-28 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231028054514/https://www.theverge.com/2023/9/19/23872888/intel-meteor-lake-core-ultra-date-chip-specs-details |url-status=live }}</ref> There is full support included for the [[DirectX#DirectX 12 Ultimate|DirectX 12 Ultimate]] graphics API and Intel's X<sup>e</sup>SS upscaler, an alternative to [[Nvidia]]'s DLSS and [[Advanced Micro Devices|AMD]]'s FSR. Intel claims that the graphics tile in Meteor Lake can give a similar level of performance to discrete graphics. Tom Petersen claimed that Meteor Lake's integrated graphics performance is "not that far from a [RTX] 3050". Intel demonstrated ''[[Dying Light 2]]'' running on Meteor Lake's integrated graphics at 1080p with X<sup>e</sup>SS performance mode upscaling from 720p.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Ridley |first1=Jacob |date=2023-09-19 |title=I didn't need a graphics card to play Dying Light 2 on Intel's new laptop chip |url=https://www.pcgamer.com/intel-meteor-lake-igpu-gaming-performance/ |website=PC Gamer |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-05-23 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240523145155/https://www.pcgamer.com/intel-meteor-lake-igpu-gaming-performance/ |url-status=live }}</ref> A hardware listing from [[Dell]] confirmed that in order to fully make use of the integrated Arc graphics, the system must be configured with at least 16GB of memory running in dual-channel mode. Not meeting the minimum memory requirements means that the system will report using lower performance "Intel Graphics" instead of "Arc" graphics.<ref>{{cite web |title=Dell confirms Intel Core Ultra 155H/125H specs, Arc iGPU requires dual-channel 16GB memory |url=https://videocardz.com/newz/dell-confirms-intel-core-ultra-155h-125h-specs-arc-igpu-requires-dual-channel-16gb-memory |website=VideoCardz |language=en-US |date=2023-11-22 |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-05-23 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240523145155/https://videocardz.com/newz/dell-confirms-intel-core-ultra-155h-125h-specs-arc-igpu-requires-dual-channel-16gb-memory |url-status=live }}</ref>

=== SoC tile ===
Meteor Lake's SoC tile is the always-active central tile that communicates with other tiles like the CPU and GPU tiles.<ref name="Alcorn 2023-09-19"/> It provides some I/O functions such as display output unit and the [[memory controller]]. Meteor Lake's memory controller is limited to supporting DDR5 and LPDDR5 memory as support for DDR4 memory is dropped. I/O components built into the SoC tile include [[Wi-Fi 6|Wi-Fi 6E]] and [[IEEE 802.11be|Wi-Fi 7]], Bluetooth 5.4, [[USB4]], USB 3.2 2x2, 8 [[Direct Media Interface|DMI 4.0]] lanes and up to four [[Thunderbolt (interface)#Thunderbolt 4|Thunderbolt 4]] ports.<ref>{{cite web |last=Hachman |first=Mark |date=2023-09-19 |title=6 ways Intel's new 14th-gen 'Meteor Lake' CPUs supercharge laptops |url=https://www.pcworld.com/article/2069640/intels-14th-gen-core-chip-meteor-lake-what-you-need-to-know.html |website=PCWorld |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2023-11-06 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231106070505/https://www.pcworld.com/article/2069640/intels-14th-gen-core-chip-meteor-lake-what-you-need-to-know.html |url-status=live }}</ref> The SoC tile is fabricated using [[TSMC]]'s [[7 nm process|N6]] node as it is more cost effective.<ref name="Alcorn 2023-09-19"/>

The SoC tile also contains two ultra low power Crestmont E-cores that Intel has dubbed a 'Low Power Island' that operates with lower voltage and lower frequency.<ref name="Bonshor">{{cite web |last=Bonshor |first=Gavin |date=2023-09-19 |title=Intel Unveils Meteor Lake Architecture: Intel 4 Heralds the Disaggregated Future of Mobile CPUs |url=https://www.anandtech.com/show/20046/intel-unveils-meteor-lake-architecture-intel-4-heralds-the-disaggregated-future-of-mobile-cpus |website=AnandTech |language=en-US |access-date=2024-05-23 |archive-date=2023-09-22 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230922065443/https://www.anandtech.com/show/20046/intel-unveils-meteor-lake-architecture-intel-4-heralds-the-disaggregated-future-of-mobile-cpus |url-status=dead }}</ref> The SoC's low power E-cores are limited in frequency to 2.5{{nbsp}}GHz compared to the 3.8{{nbsp}}GHz of the E-cores. These cores are designed to handle deep background tasks for laptops in idle or sleep mode.<ref>{{cite web |last=Liu |first=Zhiye |date=2023-05-20 |title=Intel Meteor Lake 16-Core, 14-Core CPUs Surface In New Benchmarks |url=https://www.tomshardware.com/news/intel-meteor-lake-16-core-14-core-cpus-surface-in-new-benchmarks |website=Tom's Hardware |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-05-23 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240523145156/https://www.tomshardware.com/news/intel-meteor-lake-16-core-14-core-cpus-surface-in-new-benchmarks |url-status=live }}</ref> All deep background tasks being handled by two Crestmont E-cores in the SoC tile allows the inactive CPU tile to be turned off entirely.<ref name="Shankland & Grunin"/> This is intended to reduce power consumption and extend battery life for laptops in a sleep mode state. These low power E-cores in the SoC tile are prioritised by Intel Thread Director scheduling. If work cannot be contained on the SoC E-cores, it will then be moved to the compute tile's E-cores as the next priority cores. The final priority cores are the 6 P-cores which are used when the work cannot be contained on the compute tile E-cores. The SoC's low power E-cores lack an L3 cache that the Crestmont E-cores in the compute tile have access to. If a low power E-core encounters a data miss in the L2 cache, there is no L3 cache to fall back on so it must instead search the much slower system memory for data.<ref name="C&C 2024-05-20">{{cite web |title=Comparing Crestmonts: No L3 Hurts |url=https://chipsandcheese.com/2024/05/20/comparing-crestmonts-no-l3-hurts/ |website=Chips and Cheese |language=en-US |date=2024-05-20 |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-07-17 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240717170900/https://chipsandcheese.com/2024/05/20/comparing-crestmonts-no-l3-hurts/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

==== Media engine ====
Rather than the media engine be located on the GPU tile, it is instead placed on the SoC tile so that the GPU tile does not need to be turned on when decoding video or using a display output. This arrangement enables greater power efficiency as the GPU tile is not always active while the system is at idle or under light loads like video playback. There is support added in the media engine for [[AV1]] hardware encoding up to [[8K resolution|8K]] video with 10-bit color depth.<ref>{{cite web |last=Wilson |first=Jason R. |date=2022-12-28 |title=Intel Meteor Lake Confirmed To Offer AV1 Encoding & Decoding Support |url=https://wccftech.com/intel-meteor-lake-confirmed-to-offer-av1-encoding-decoding-support/ |website=Wccftech |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-05-23 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240523145155/https://wccftech.com/intel-meteor-lake-confirmed-to-offer-av1-encoding-decoding-support/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Four display pipes provide support for [[HDMI#Version 2.1|HDMI 2.1]] and [[DisplayPort#2.1|DisplayPort 2.1]] UHBR20 display outputs with the ability to drive up to four [[4K resolution|4K]] 60{{nbsp}}Hz [[High dynamic range|HDR]] monitors at once or one 8K [[High dynamic range|HDR]] monitor. [[1080p]] and [[1440p]] monitors can be supported with a refresh rate up to 360{{nbsp}}Hz.<ref name="Shankland & Grunin">{{cite web |last1=Shankland |first1=Stephen |last2=Grunin |first2=Lori |date=2023-09-19 |title=Intel Tucks Battery-Saving Tricks Into Its Meteor Lake PC Processor |url=https://www.cnet.com/tech/computing/intel-tucks-battery-saving-tricks-into-its-meteor-lake-pc-processor/ |website=CNET |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-07-16 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240716124234/https://www.cnet.com/tech/computing/intel-tucks-battery-saving-tricks-into-its-meteor-lake-pc-processor/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

The I/O extender tile is the smallest tile in Meteor Lake, fabricated on [[TSMC]]'s [[7 nm process|N6]] node.<ref>{{cite web |last=Bonshor |first=Gavin |date=2023-09-19 |title=I/O Tile: Extended and Scalable Depending on Segment |url=https://www.anandtech.com/show/20046/intel-unveils-meteor-lake-architecture-intel-4-heralds-the-disaggregated-future-of-mobile-cpus/6 |website=AnandTech |language=en-US |access-date=2024-05-23 |archive-date=2024-07-16 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240716124739/https://www.anandtech.com/show/20046/intel-unveils-meteor-lake-architecture-intel-4-heralds-the-disaggregated-future-of-mobile-cpus/6 |url-status=dead }}</ref> It provides scalable I/O blocks, which is primarily to offer additional connectivity to that of the SoC tile, such as [[PCI Express#PCIe 5.0|PCIe 5.0]] lanes. === Foveros interposer base tile ===
Meteor Lake uses a passive silicon interposer placed underneath its tiles as an interconnect. The tiles are placed on top of the interposer and are bonded to the interposer using [[through-silicon via]] (TSV) connections through the two vertically stacked pieces of silicon. The TSVs connect the dies with a 36&nbsp;μm pitch to enable die-to-die communication. Placing logic dies on top of an interposer requires TSVs to connect the top dies through the interposer onto the package. By contrast, [[Advanced Micro Devices|AMD]]'s chiplet approach uses multiple pieces of silicon that are interconnected via traces on the package substrate. The benefit of AMD's approach is its cost-effective scalability where the same CCDs can be used in both their [[Ryzen]] desktop and [[Epyc]] server processors. AMD's Infinity Fabric approach comes with the drawbacks of increased [[Latency (engineering)|latency]] and using additional power for die-to-die communication at around 1.5 picojoules per bit.<ref name="Alcorn 2023-09-19"/> Intel's communication via a silicon interposer uses less power, at around 0.3 picojoules per bit, but is more expensive to produce, is less scalable and packaging is more complex.<ref name="Bonshor"/> The Foveros interposer base tile is estimated to be 23.1mm × 11.5mm in dimensions with a total die area of 265.65mm<sup>2</sup>.<ref name="Zuhair"/>

However, Meteor Lake processors are not the first Intel processors to utilize vertical die stacking with a base tile. In June 2020, Intel launched Lakefield ultra-low power mobile processors with a 7W TDP. Lakefield used Foveros packaging with a 22 nm base tile and 10 nm compute tile.<ref>{{cite web |last=Hachman |first=Mark |date=2020-06-10 |title=Intel announces its first new 'Lakefield' chips for the thinnest, lightest PCs |url=https://www.pcworld.com/article/399262/intel-announces-its-first-new-lakefield-chips-for-the-thinnest-lightest-pcs.html |website=PCWorld |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-05-23 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240523145155/https://www.pcworld.com/article/399262/intel-announces-its-first-new-lakefield-chips-for-the-thinnest-lightest-pcs.html |url-status=live }}</ref> The compute tile contained heterogenous cores with one [[Sunny Cove (microarchitecture)|Sunny Cove]] big core and four [[Tremont (microarchitecture)|Tremont]] small cores, predecessors to Meteor Lake's Redwood Cove and Crestmont cores.<ref>{{cite web |last=Cutress |first=Ian |date=2020-07-02 |title=The Intel Lakefield Deep Dive: Everything To Know About the First x86 Hybrid CPU |url=https://www.anandtech.com/show/15877/intel-hybrid-cpu-lakefield-all-you-need-to-know |website=AnandTech |language=en-US |access-date=2024-05-23 |archive-date=2024-07-16 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240716124740/https://www.anandtech.com/show/15877/intel-hybrid-cpu-lakefield-all-you-need-to-know |url-status=dead }}</ref> Lakefield was discontinued in July 2021.<ref>{{cite web |last=Hruska |first=Joel |date=2021-07-08 |title=Intel Discontinues Lakefield, Its First x86 Hybrid CPU |url=https://www.extremetech.com/computing/324435-intel-eols-lakefield-its-first-x86-hybrid-cpu |website=ExtremeTech |language=en-US |access-date=2026-05-27 |archive-date=2024-05-23 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240523145155/https://www.extremetech.com/computing/324435-intel-eols-lakefield-its-first-x86-hybrid-cpu |url-status=live }}</ref>

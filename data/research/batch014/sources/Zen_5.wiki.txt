== Background ==
Zen 5 was first officially mentioned during AMD's ''Ryzen Processors: One Year Later'' presentation on April 9, 2018.<ref>{{cite web |title=Ryzen Processors: One Year Later |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iQ_4C2TKHQ0 |website=YouTube |language=en-US |date=April 9, 2018 |access-date=August 23, 2024}}</ref>

A roadmap shown during AMD's Financial Analyst Day on June 9, 2022 confirmed that Zen 5 and Zen 5c would be launching in 3&nbsp;nm and 4&nbsp;nm variants in 2024.<ref name="STH_FAD_2022">{{cite web |title=AMD FAD 2022 AMD CPU Core Roadmap To Zen 5 |url=https://www.servethehome.com/amd-technology-roadmap-from-amd-financial-analyst-day-2022/amd-fad-2022-amd-cpu-core-roadmap-to-zen-5/ |website=ServeTheHome |language=en-US |access-date=June 3, 2024}}</ref> The earliest details on the Zen 5 architecture promised a "re-pipelined front end and wide issue" with "integrated AI and Machine Learning optimizations".<ref name="STH_FAD_2022" />

During AMD's Q4 2023 earnings call on January 30, 2024, AMD CEO [[Lisa Su]] stated that Zen 5 products would be "coming in the second half of the year".<ref>{{cite web |title=AMD reaffirms Ryzen CPUs with Zen5 architecture are coming in the second half of 2024 |url=https://videocardz.com/newz/amd-reaffirms-ryzen-cpus-with-zen5-architecture-are-coming-in-the-second-half-of-2024 |website=VideoCardz |language=en-US |date=January 31, 2024 |access-date=June 3, 2024 |archive-date=June 3, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240603153947/https://videocardz.com/newz/amd-reaffirms-ryzen-cpus-with-zen5-architecture-are-coming-in-the-second-half-of-2024 |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Architecture ==
[[File:AMD@4nmCCD(6nmIOD)@Zen5@Granite Ridge@Ryzen 5 9600X@100-000001405 BY 2429SUY 9AEQ579S40073 DSCx14 CCD poly@5xExt.jpg|thumb|alt=Die-Shot of an AMD Ryzen 5 9600X, mainly in blue, and rather symmetric structure|Die-Shot of an AMD Ryzen 5 9600X with a Zen 5 microarchitecture]]
Zen 5 is a ground-up redesign of [[Zen 4]] with a wider front-end, increased floating-point throughput, and more-accurate branch prediction.<ref>{{cite web |last=Bonshor |first=Gavin |date=June 2, 2024 |title=AMD Unveils Ryzen 9000 CPUs For Desktop, Zen 5 Takes Center Stage at Computex 2024 |url=https://www.anandtech.com/show/21415/amd-unveils-ryzen-9000-cpus-for-desktop-zen-5-takes-center-stage-at-computex-2024 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20240603051702/https://www.anandtech.com/show/21415/amd-unveils-ryzen-9000-cpus-for-desktop-zen-5-takes-center-stage-at-computex-2024 |url-status=dead |archive-date=June 3, 2024 |website=AnandTech |language=en-US |access-date=June 3, 2024}}</ref>

=== Fabrication process ===
Zen 5 was designed with both 4&nbsp;nm and 3&nbsp;nm processes in mind. This acted as an insurance policy for AMD in the event that TSMC's mass production of its N3 nodes were to face delays, significant wafer defect issues, or capacity issues. One industry analyst estimated early N3 wafer yields to be at 55% while others estimated yields to be similar to those of N5 at between 60-80%.<ref>{{cite web |last=Norem |first=Josh |date=July 14, 2023 |title=Analyst: TSMC Hitting 55% Yields on 3nm Node for Apple's A17 Bionic, M3 SoCs |url=https://www.extremetech.com/computing/analyst-tsmc-hitting-55-yields-on-3nm-node-for-apples-a17-bionic-m3-socs |website=ExtremeTech |language=en-US |access-date=June 3, 2024}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Shilov |first=Anton |date=December 31, 2022 |title=Analysts Estimate TSMC's 3nm Yields Between 60% and 80% |url=https://www.tomshardware.com/news/analysts-estimate-tsmc-n3-yields-between-60-and-80-percent |website=Tom's Hardware |language=en-US |access-date=June 3, 2024}}</ref> Additionally, [[Apple Inc.|Apple]], as TSMC's largest customer, gets priority access to the latest process nodes. In 2022, Apple was responsible for 23% of TSMC's $72 billion in total revenue.<ref>{{cite web |last=Norem |first=Josh |date=August 8, 2023 |title=Apple Bought All of TSMC's 3nm Capacity for an Entire Year |url=https://www.extremetech.com/computing/apple-bought-all-of-tsmcs-3nm-capacity-for-an-entire-year |website=ExtremeTech |language=en-US |access-date=June 3, 2024 |archive-date=June 3, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240603153947/https://www.extremetech.com/computing/apple-bought-all-of-tsmcs-3nm-capacity-for-an-entire-year |url-status=live }}</ref> After N3 began ramping at the end of 2022, Apple bought up the entirety of TSMC's early N3B wafer production capacity to fabricate their A17 and M3 SoCs.<ref>{{cite web |last=Norem |first=Josh |date=April 27, 2023 |title=TSMC Says It Can't Keep Up With Apple's Demands for 3nm Wafers |url=https://www.extremetech.com/computing/tsmc-says-it-cant-keep-up-with-apples-demands-for-3nm-wafers |website=ExtremeTech |language=en-US |access-date=June 3, 2024 |archive-date=June 3, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240603153948/https://www.extremetech.com/computing/tsmc-says-it-cant-keep-up-with-apples-demands-for-3nm-wafers |url-status=live }}</ref> Zen 5 desktop and server processors continue to use the N6 node for the I/O die fabrication.<ref name=":0">{{cite web |last=Bonshor |first=Gavin |date=2024-06-02 |title=AMD Unveils Ryzen 9000 CPUs For Desktop, Zen 5 Takes Center Stage at Computex 2024 |url=https://www.anandtech.com/show/21415/amd-unveils-ryzen-9000-cpus-for-desktop-zen-5-takes-center-stage-at-computex-2024 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240603033848/https://www.anandtech.com/show/21415/amd-unveils-ryzen-9000-cpus-for-desktop-zen-5-takes-center-stage-at-computex-2024 |archive-date=2024-06-03 |access-date=2024-06-02 |work=AnandTech |language=en-US}}</ref>

Zen 5 Core Complex Dies (CCDs) are fabricated on TSMC's N4X node which is intended to accommodate higher frequencies for high-performance computing (HPC) applications.<ref>{{cite web |last=Norem |first=Josh |date=July 22, 2024 |title=AMD's Zen 5 Architectures Boast a 28% Increase in Density Over Zen 4 |url=https://www.extremetech.com/computing/amds-zen-5-architectures-boast-a-28-increase-in-density-over-zen-4 |website=ExtremeTech |language=en-US |access-date=September 16, 2024}}</ref> Zen 4-based mobile processors were fabricated on the N4P node which is targeted more toward power efficiency. N4X maintains IP compatibility with N4P and offers a 6% frequency gain over N4P at the same power but comes with the trade-off of moderate leakage.<ref>{{cite web |title=Advanced Technologies for HPC: N4/N4P/N4X |url=https://www.tsmc.com/english/dedicatedFoundry/technology/platform_HPC_tech_advancedTech |website=TSMC |language=en |access-date=June 3, 2024}}</ref> Compared to the N5 node used to produce Zen 4 CCDs, N4X can enable up to 15% higher frequencies while running at 1.2V.<ref>{{cite web |last=Shilov |first=Anton |date=December 17, 2021 |title=TSMC Unveils N4X Node: Extreme High-Performance at High Voltages |url=https://www.anandtech.com/show/17123/tsmc-unveils-n4x-node-high-voltages-for-high-clocks |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211218013526/https://www.anandtech.com/show/17123/tsmc-unveils-n4x-node-high-voltages-for-high-clocks |url-status=dead |archive-date=December 18, 2021 |website=AnandTech |language=en-US |access-date=June 3, 2024}}</ref>

The Zen 5 CCD, codenamed "Eldora", has a [[Die (integrated circuit)|die]] size of 70.6mm<sup>2</sup>, a 0.5% reduction in area from Zen 4's 71mm<sup>2</sup> CCD while achieving a 28% increase in transistor density due to the N4X process node.<ref name="TPU 2024-07-16">{{cite web |title=AMD Granite Ridge and Strix Point Zen 5 Die-sizes and Transistor Counts Confirmed |url=https://www.techpowerup.com/324562/amd-granite-ridge-and-strix-point-zen-5-die-sizes-and-transistor-counts-confirmed |website=TechPowerUp |language=en |date=July 16, 2024 |access-date=September 16, 2024 |archive-date=February 1, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250201175408/https://www.techpowerup.com/324562/amd-granite-ridge-and-strix-point-zen-5-die-sizes-and-transistor-counts-confirmed |url-status=live }}</ref> Zen 5's CCD contains 8.315 billion transistors compared to the Zen 4 CCD's 6.5 billion transistors.<ref>{{cite web |last=Shilov |first=Anton |date=July 18, 2024 |title=AMD's Zen 5 chips pack in 8.315 billion transistors per compute die, a 28% increase in density |url=https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/amds-zen-5-chips-pack-in-8315-billion-transistors-per-compute-die-a-28-increase-in-density |website=Tom's Hardware |language=en-US |access-date=September 16, 2024 |archive-date=January 21, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250121120926/https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/amds-zen-5-chips-pack-in-8315-billion-transistors-per-compute-die-a-28-increase-in-density |url-status=live }}</ref> One Zen 5 core is larger than one Zen 4 core, but the CCD has been reduced via shrinking the L3 cache. The monolithic die used by "Strix Point" mobile processors, fabricated on TSMC's lower power N4P node, measures 232.5mm<sup>2</sup> in area.<ref name="TPU 2024-07-16"/>

===Front end===
====Branch prediction====
Zen 5's changes to [[branch predictor|branch prediction]] are the most significant divergence from any previous Zen microarchitecture. The branch predictor in a core tries to predict the outcome when there are diverging code paths.
Zen 5's branch predictor is able to operate two-ahead where it can predict up to two branches per clock cycle. Previous architectures were limited to one branch instruction per clock cycle, limiting the instruction-fetch throughput of branch-heavy programs.<ref>{{cite web |last=Posch |first=Maya |date=July 28, 2024 |title=AMD Returns to 1996 With Zen 5's Two-Block Ahead Branch Predictor |url=https://hackaday.com/2024/07/28/amd-returns-to-1996-with-zen-5s-two-block-ahead-branch-predictor/ |website=Hackaday |language=en-US |access-date=September 16, 2024}}</ref> Two-ahead branch predictors have been discussed in academic research dating back to André Seznec ''et al''.'s 1996 paper "Multiple-block ahead branch predictors".<ref>{{cite book |last1=Seznec |first1=André |last2=Jourdan |first2=Stéphan |last3=Sainrat |first3=Pascal |last4=Michaud |first4=Pierre |chapter=Multiple-block ahead branch predictors |year=1996 |title=Proceedings of the seventh international conference on Architectural support for programming languages and operating systems |chapter-url=https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/237090.237169 |pages=116–127 |language=en-US |doi=10.1145/237090.237169 |isbn=0-89791-767-7 |archive-date=September 16, 2024 |access-date=September 16, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240916104002/https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/237090.237169 |url-status=live }}</ref> 28 years after it was first proposed in academic research, AMD's Zen 5 architecture became the first microarchitecture to fully implement two-ahead branch prediction.<ref name="TH" /> Increased data prefetching assists the branch predictor.<ref name="TH" />

===Execution engines===

====Integer units====
Zen 5 contains six Arithmetic Logic Units (ALUs), up from four ALUs in prior Zen architectures. A greater number of ALUs that handle common integer operations can increase per-cycle scalar integer throughput by 50%.<ref>{{cite web |last=Lam |first=Chester |date=October 8, 2023 |title=Zen 5's Leaked Slides |url=https://chipsandcheese.com/2023/10/08/zen-5s-leaked-slides/ |website=Chips and Cheese |language=en-US |access-date=June 3, 2024}}</ref>

====Vector engines and instructions====
The vector engine in Zen 5 features four floating-point pipes compared to three pipes in Zen 4. Zen 4 introduced AVX-512 instructions. AVX-512 capabilities have been expanded with Zen 5 with a doubling of the floating-point pipe width to a native 512-bit floating-point datapath. The AVX-512 datapath is configurable depending on the product. Ryzen 9000 series desktop processors and EPYC 9005 server processors feature the full 512-bit datapath, but Ryzen AI 300/400 mobile processors feature a 256-bit datapath to reduce power consumption. AVX-512 instructions have been extended with the VP2INTERSECT extension, in addition to 256-bit AVX-VNNI. Additionally, there is greater <code>bfloat16</code> throughput.<ref name="TH" />

=== Cache ===
==== L1 ====
The wider front end in the Zen 5 architecture necessitates larger caches and higher memory bandwidth in order to keep the cores fed with data. The L1 cache per core is increased from 64&nbsp;KB to 80&nbsp;KB per core. The L1 instruction cache remains the same at 32&nbsp;KB but the L1 data cache is increased from 32&nbsp;KB to 48&nbsp;KB per core. Furthermore, the bandwidth of the L1 data cache for 512-bit floating-point unit pipes has also been doubled. The L1 data cache's associativity has increased from 8-way to 12-way in order to accommodate its larger size.<ref name="TH" />

==== L2 ====
The L2 cache remains at 1&nbsp;MB but its associativity has increased from 8-way to 16-way. Zen 5 also has a doubled L2 cache bandwidth of 64 bytes per clock.<ref name="TH" />

==== L3 ====
The L3 cache is filled from L2 [[victim cache|cache victims]] and in-flight misses. Latency for accessing the L3 cache has been reduced by 3.5 cycles.<ref>{{cite web |last=Kennedy |first=Patrick |date=August 27, 2024 |title=AMD Zen 5 Core is at Hot Chips 2024 |url=https://www.servethehome.com/amd-zen-5-core-is-at-hot-chips-2024/ |website=ServeTheHome |language=en-US |access-date=September 16, 2024}}</ref> A Zen 5 Core Complex Die (CCD) contains 32&nbsp;MB of L3 cache shared between the 8 cores. In Zen 5 3D V-Cache CCDs, a piece of silicon containing 64&nbsp;MB of extra L3 cache is placed under the cores rather than on top like in prior generations for a total of 96&nbsp;MB.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Lilly |first1=Paul |date=October 31, 2024 |title=AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D Unveiled With A 3D V-Cache Tweak For Gamers And Overclockers |url=https://hothardware.com/news/amd-ryzen-7-9800x3d-kicks-off-zen-5-3d-v-cache-party |website=HotHardware |language=en-US |access-date=November 1, 2024 |archive-date=November 7, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241107073921/https://hothardware.com/news/amd-ryzen-7-9800x3d-kicks-off-zen-5-3d-v-cache-party |url-status=live }}</ref> This allows for increased core frequency compared to previous generation 3D V-Cache implementations which were sensitive to higher voltages. The Zen 5-based Ryzen 7 9800X3D has a 500&nbsp;MHz increased base frequency over the Zen 4-based Ryzen 7 7800X3D and allows overclocking for the first time.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Tyson |first1=Mark |date=October 31, 2024 |title=AMD crowns the Ryzen 7 9800X3D a 'gaming legend' in a surprise announcement — chipmaker claims $479 Zen 5 3D V-Cache chip is up to an average 20% faster than Intel Core Ultra 9 flagship |url=https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/amd-crowns-the-ryzen-7-9800x3d-a-gaming-legend-in-a-surprise-announcement-chipmaker-claims-usd479-zen-5-3d-v-cache-chip-is-up-to-an-average-20-percent-faster-than-intel-core-ultra-9-flagship |website=Tom's Hardware |language=en-US |access-date=November 1, 2024 |archive-date=November 1, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241101042123/https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/amd-crowns-the-ryzen-7-9800x3d-a-gaming-legend-in-a-surprise-announcement-chipmaker-claims-usd479-zen-5-3d-v-cache-chip-is-up-to-an-average-20-percent-faster-than-intel-core-ultra-9-flagship |url-status=live }}</ref>

Ryzen AI 300 APUs, codenamed "Strix Point", features 24&nbsp;MB of total L3 cache which is split into two separate cache arrays. 16&nbsp;MB of dedicated L3 cache is shared by the 4 Zen 5 cores and 8&nbsp;MB is shared by the 8 Zen 5c cores.<ref>{{cite web |title=AMD's Strix Point: Zen 5 Hits Mobile |url=https://chipsandcheese.com/2024/08/10/amds-strix-point-zen-5-hits-mobile/ |website=Chips and Cheese |language=en-US |date=August 10, 2024 |access-date=September 16, 2024 |archive-date=September 15, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240915213355/https://chipsandcheese.com/2024/08/10/amds-strix-point-zen-5-hits-mobile/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Zen 5c cores are not able to access the 16&nbsp;MB L3 cache array and vice versa.<ref>{{cite web |title=AMD Strix Point SoC Reintroduces Dual-CCX CPU, Other Interesting Silicon Details Revealed |url=https://www.techpowerup.com/324872/amd-strix-point-soc-reintroduces-dual-ccx-cpu-other-interesting-silicon-details-revealed |website=TechPowerUp |language=en |date=July 24, 2024 |access-date=September 16, 2024 |archive-date=September 16, 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240916105504/https://www.techpowerup.com/324872/amd-strix-point-soc-reintroduces-dual-ccx-cpu-other-interesting-silicon-details-revealed |url-status=live }}</ref>

==== Strix Halo ====
Strix Halo represented a major departure of previous multi-chiplet mobile processors. Strix Halo's CCD is actually not the same type of CCD used on regular Zen 5 desktop processors. Instead it features a unique "sea of wires" interconnect that replaces the previous infinity fabric. This change allowed for greater power efficiency but also greater design complexity. This type of interconnect is rumored to be on Zen 6, the next generation of AMD CPU microarchitecture.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Zuhair |first=Muhammad |date=2025-09-28 |title=AMD to Pivot from SERDES to a "Sea-of-Wires" D2D Interconnect in Next-Gen Zen 6 CPUs, Bringing Major Power-Efficiency and Latency Gains |url=https://wccftech.com/amd-to-pivot-from-serdes-to-a-sea-of-wires-d2d-interconnect-with-zen-6-cpus/ |access-date=2025-10-03 |website=Wccftech |language=en-US |archive-date=September 30, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250930155556/https://wccftech.com/amd-to-pivot-from-serdes-to-a-sea-of-wires-d2d-interconnect-with-zen-6-cpus/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=maH6KZ0YkXU |title=How AMD is re-thinking Chiplet Design |date=2025-09-27 |last=High Yield |access-date=2025-10-03 |via=YouTube |archive-date=October 3, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251003103508/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=maH6KZ0YkXU |url-status=live }}</ref>

Common features of Strix Halo mobile CPUs:<ref>{{Cite web |last=Discuss |first=AleksandarK |date=2025-02-18 |title=AMD Ryzen AI Max+ "Strix Halo" Die Exposed and Annotated |url=https://www.techpowerup.com/332745/amd-ryzen-ai-max-strix-halo-die-exposed-and-annotated |access-date=2025-10-03 |website=TechPowerUp |language=en}}</ref>

* Socket: BGA, FP 11 package type.
* All CPUs only support soldered LPDDR5X memory with a 256-bit memory bus.
* All CPUs support 16 lanes of PCIe 4.0 lanes.
* iGPU uses the RDNA 3.5 architecture.
* Fabrication process: TSMC N4P FinFET.

==== Turin ====
Alongside Granite Ridge desktop and Strix Point mobile processors, the Epyc 9005 series of high-performance server processors, codenamed ''Turin'', were also announced at Computex on June 3, 2024. It uses the same [[Socket SP5|SP5]] socket as the previous Epyc 9004 series processors, and packs up to 128 cores and 256 threads on the top-end model. ==== Turin Dense ====
A variant of Epyc 9005 using Zen 5c (''"Prometheus"'') cores was also shown off at Computex. It features a maximum of 192 cores and 384 threads, and is manufactured on a [[3 nm]] process.<ref name="tomshw-epyc9005-computex" />

== Zen 5c ==
'''Zen 5c''' (''"Prometheus"'') is a compact variant of the Zen 5 (''"Nirvana"'')<ref name="Zen 5 Nirvana"/> core, primarily targeted at hyperscale cloud compute server customers.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Smith |first=Ryan |date=June 9, 2022 |title=AMD Zen Architecture Roadmap: Zen 5 in 2024 With All-New Microarchitecture |url=https://www.anandtech.com/show/17439/amd-zen-architecture-roadmap-zen-5-in-2024-with-allnew-microarchitecture |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220609202748/https://www.anandtech.com/show/17439/amd-zen-architecture-roadmap-zen-5-in-2024-with-allnew-microarchitecture |url-status=dead |archive-date=June 9, 2022 |website=AnandTech |language=en-US |access-date=December 11, 2022}}</ref> It succeeds the Zen 4c (''"Dionysus"'') core.
